Welcome to your Wellbeing Profile, from Tools for the Tough Days!

This questionnaire explores how you’ve been feeling, how you respond on tough days, and what helps you.

We’ll use what you share to create your Wellbeing Profile. It’s a starting point to understand how you respond in different situations, and consider what might support your wellbeing.

As you answer, think about your life as it is right now. Your responses may vary depending on what’s happening and how you’re feeling. They can also change over time.

To help you understand yourself a little better, we use three characters as metaphors.

The Bodyguard represents ways of protecting yourself or getting through difficulty, such as pushing through, keeping busy or pulling back.

The Teenager represents feelings and emotional needs. It can bring energy, excitement and enthusiasm, as well as hurt, frustration or a wish for connection.

And the Adult represents your capacity to pause, consider different perspectives, and choose a response.

Imagine these three travelling together in the same car. Each brings something useful. In different situations, one or another may take the wheel, depending on what’s happening and how you’re feeling.

These are responses we can all recognise in ourselves. They aren’t labels for your age, maturity or whole personality.

The aim is to notice who’s at the wheel and consider what you need, with more room to choose your next step. When you’re ready, let’s begin!

It’s helpful to know that this description feels familiar. Recognising a response is a starting point. When you learn to identify your patterns, you can notice them earlier, understand what you need and have more room to choose a different response. That gives you a practical starting point for making the changes you want in your life.

Your primary driver describes the response that may take the wheel on tough days.

Your answers may also identify a secondary driver: a distinct response that may come forward on really tough days, when things feel especially difficult or you’re running on empty.

Understanding both can help you recognise how your responses may change as things get harder, and consider what support you might need.

As a general example, someone might keep going and hold everything together at first, then pull back and go quiet when they become exhausted. Understanding that shift can help them recognise what they need sooner.

In our car analogy, that might mean a different character taking the wheel, or a different response from the same character.

If you choose to purchase the Wellbeing Bundle, your personalised full report will explain your secondary driver, where your answers identify one, and how it may work alongside your primary driver.

The guided 90-day wellbeing journey gives you a structured way to build that understanding and practise small changes, with the goals you selected helping connect the journey to what matters to you.

You begin by noticing. In the first week, you look for familiar patterns in everyday moments: what’s happening, how you’re feeling, how you respond and what you might need.

Over the following weeks, you gradually explore those responses and try simple, practical actions. Weekly activities give you time to reflect on what’s working and consider what you’d like to adjust.

As the journey continues, you bring useful options together in your Tough-Day Guide, so you have familiar tools to return to on tough days. You also build one manageable wellbeing practice to carry forward.

To help you return to the journey, you choose a reminder time that connects with something you already do.

For example, if you usually have a morning cuppa, you might choose that as your reminder time. At your chosen time, you’ll receive a notification inviting you to open that day’s guided step.

When you open your daily guided step, you’ll begin with a brief mood check-in. Using the mood slider, you can record how you’re feeling that day.

Your check-ins are saved so you can look back over time, notice changes and recognise patterns in how you’ve been feeling.

Some days may feel easier and others tougher. Looking back can help you reflect on those changes alongside what you’ve been learning and practising.

You then continue to that day’s insight and practical action. Each guided step builds on what came before, giving you a structured way to move through the journey.

You’ll also have access to over 370 resources on Tools for the Tough Days, to read or listen to while your platform access is active.
