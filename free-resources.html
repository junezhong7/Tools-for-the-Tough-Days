Anxiety and Avoidance - Breaking the Cycle

First tip Every time we avoid something because of anxiety, we send our brain a message: "that thing really was dangerous." And so the anxiety around it grows.
Read the full preview ▾
How the Avoidance Cycle Works
  • Something triggers anxiety - a situation, a thought, a feeling
  • The anxiety feels uncomfortable or threatening
  • You avoid the situation to get relief
  • Relief comes - briefly
  • The next time the situation comes up, the anxiety is a little bigger
  • The avoidance becomes a little more automatic

Over time, the world that feels safe gets smaller and smaller. And the anxiety gets louder.

What Avoidance Can Look Like

Avoidance isn't always obvious. It can be physically avoiding places, putting off decisions, seeking constant reassurance, or distraction - scrolling, overworking, keeping busy...

Breaking the Cycle - Gradually

The key is gradual, repeated exposure - facing the thing that triggers anxiety in small, manageable steps. Each time you face something and discover you survived it, your brain updates its threat assessment...

1. Identify one thing you've been avoiding. 2. Break it into the smallest possible steps. 3. Face the smallest step, and stay until the anxiety reduces...
